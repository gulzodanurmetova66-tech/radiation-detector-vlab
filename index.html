2. Predict → simulate → explain

Case A — non-paralyzable count loss

At 1000 incoming counts/s and τ = 150 μs, predict the observed rate before changing any sliders.

r/(1+rτ) = 1000/(1+0.15) = 869.565 cps. The observed count is smaller because events during dead time are not recorded. This is not a measurement from a real instrument.
Case B — selecting a type of detector

A fictional monitoring task requires pulse-height information related to deposited energy. Which regime is more appropriate in principle: proportional or Geiger?

Within its suitable operating regime, a proportional counter preserves a relation to primary ionization. Conventional GM pulses generally do not represent the energy deposited by each event. Real instrumentation requires specialist specifications and calibration.
Case C — rate turnover

Increase the incident event rate beyond 1/τ using the paralyzable model. Why can the recorded rate fall even while input rate increases?

Every new event inside dead time extends the blind interval. Under the idealized paralyzable assumption, the observed rate peaks at incident r=1/τ and decreases above this value.
Case D — distinguish approximation from calibration

Is the shape of the voltage-response curve sufficient to establish the correct operating voltage of a physical radiation detector?

No. These traces are pedagogical surrogates. Physical operating limits depend on gas mixture, geometry, quenching, electronics and manufacturer validation.
